The rules the plan follows

  • Less hard running. About 4 in 10 of my recent runs were hard. The plan keeps two quality sessions a week and makes everything else easy. It's informed by Stöggl & Sperlich [3], but with a weekly threshold session it's closer to a pyramidal mix than a strictly polarized one.
  • No single-run spikes. No run is more than 10% longer than my longest run in the previous 30 days, following Frandsen et al. [2].
  • Gentle returns. After each recovery week, volume comes back to about 90% of the last full week rather than jumping straight to the old peak [1].
  • A real taper. The last two weeks average about 45% less distance than week 10, with the intensity kept, in line with Bosquet et al. [4].

Method and sources

How the data was handled

  • Source: my Strava “Download your account” export. Only runs from 2025–2026 are used, and only date, distance, moving time, grade-adjusted distance and heart rate are kept. No routes, maps, start times or activity names are published.
  • Dates are Strava's UTC dates. New Zealand is ahead of UTC, so a Saturday-morning parkrun shows up on the Friday.
  • Runs are classed as hard (average HR ≥ 158 or max HR ≥ 195), long (15 km or more) or easy. Race efforts always count as hard. Heart rate comes from a wrist sensor, which can lag or spike during hard efforts, so the hard/easy split is approximate. Runs before June 2026 have no heart rate.
  • Runs slower than 7:00/km grade-adjusted are left out of the efficiency chart (mixed hike/run outings).
  • One fix: on 4 March 2026 the moving time is broken by a GPS glitch, so elapsed time is used.
  • The export has no splits, so the 22 October kilometre splits are entered separately. Their distance was checked by counting laps.
